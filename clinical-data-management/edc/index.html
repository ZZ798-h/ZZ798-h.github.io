Background

What Is EDC?

Electronic Data Capture

An Electronic Data Capture (EDC) system is used to collect and manage clinical research data in structured electronic case report forms (eCRFs). It helps study teams enter, review, query, correct, and prepare data for downstream analysis.

Why It Matters

EDC workflows support consistency, traceability, and timely review. The quality of field definitions, form design, edit checks, and query handling directly affects the reliability of the final clinical dataset.

Process

Practice Workflow

The workflow follows the data from source review through reviewable output. Each step creates a natural point for a screenshot or short note.

  1. Review context Read the fictional protocol, source pages, subject status, and visit schedule.
  2. Select the correct casebook Confirm the subject, event/visit, and form before entering data.
  3. Enter the eCRF Transfer fictional source information into the matching structured fields.
  4. Save and review checks Save the form and inspect any required-field, range, or consistency messages.
  5. Handle queries Investigate the discrepancy and document a clear response or correction.
  6. Confirm traceability Review audit history and exported practice data for completeness.

Reflection

Key Takeaways

  • Correct subject, visit, and form selection is a data-quality control.
  • eCRF structure determines how consistently study information can be collected.
  • Edit checks identify potential issues, but human review is still needed to interpret context.
  • Query responses should be concise, evidence-based, and traceable.
  • Audit trails and exports connect operational data entry with QC and downstream analysis.

Next Step

Build a Small Synthetic Clinical Dataset

The next artifact will convert the workflow into three simple fictional CSV datasets: Demographics (DM), Vital Signs (VS), and Adverse Events (AE). I will then run basic SAS QC checks for missing values, invalid dates, out-of-range measurements, and cross-file consistency, recording any findings in a query log.

DM, VS, and AE are common domain abbreviations. At this stage, the goal is not to claim a submission-ready CDISC package; it is to demonstrate the logic connecting collected data, cleaning rules, documentation, and programming.
